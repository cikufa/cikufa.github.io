About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at the University at Buffalo, working with Prof. Karthik Dantu in the Distributed Robotics and Networked Embedded Sensing (DRONES) Lab.

My research focuses on active perception and perception-aware planning for robotic autonomy. I am interested in how robots can actively control what and how they sense to improve downstream localization, planning, and manipulation, rather than treating perception as a fixed input stream.

My recent work studies online viewpoint optimization for motion-decoupled cameras, adaptive illumination for visual SLAM, and controlled photometric variation for robot vision. I am currently exploring task-aware active perception for long-horizon manipulation under partial observability, especially sensing actions that help satisfy the preconditions of downstream skills.

More broadly, I am interested in robot learning, 3D vision, visual localization, world models, and building perception-action loops that remain useful under real-world sensing constraints.
